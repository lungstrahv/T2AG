## What this means in practice

You may copy, redistribute, adapt and build upon the prose, for any purpose
including commercially, provided that you:

1. **Attribute** — credit MIKP (t2ac) as the original author, link to the

2. **ShareAlike** — distribute your adaptations of the prose under CC BY-SA 4.0
   as well.

## Why the prose is copyleft and the code is not

This split is deliberate, and it is the opposite of the usual arrangement.

The code is the smaller half. Tools get rewritten; a fork that reimplements
`t2ag_doctor.py` in another language has not taken anything from anyone.
Apache-2.0 leaves that free, and its §6 keeps the *name* T2AG attached to the
author's own line — which is the part worth protecting when the author's
livelihood depends on being identifiable as the source.

The prose is the actual work. The constitution, the playbooks, the discipline
about carriers and closure, the reasons written next to each decision — that
is where the years went, and it is what a platform could absorb silently. The
ShareAlike condition does not prevent absorption; nothing does. What it does
is make absorption *visible*: an adaptation that stays closed is a licence
violation, so the choice to take without giving back has to be made in the
open rather than by default.

Neither licence is a fence. They are both, mostly, a way of stating what this
project considers fair — legible to the next person who has to decide what to
do with a copy.
